B — Typography

Motion

  • Reveal on scroll — fade 0→1 and rise 26 px → 0, over 1.05 s, easing cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1). Triggers when the element is 12% into the viewport.
  • Stagger — 70–100 ms between siblings in a grid. Never more than five steps.
  • Hero parallax — background moves at 0.16× scroll speed, image pre-scaled 1.02 so no edge shows.
  • Image hover — scale 1.045–1.06 over 1.5 s. Slow is the point.
  • Nav — transparent over the hero; after 60 px of scroll it fades to rgba(2,56,61,.94) with an 18 px backdrop blur.
  • Banned — slide-ins from the side, bounce, spin, zoom-on-load, typewriter, counters that tick up.

F — Photography

The one thing still missing.

Every image in V1 is an abstract Arizona-palette placeholder. They are composed to hold the layout and to look intentional rather than empty, but they are not the finished design. Photography is what will move this site from “well made” to “real”.

Do not use: generic office workers, handshakes, people in suits pointing at charts, world maps, circuit-board graphics, glowing network overlays, AI-generated illustration, or any image where you cannot say where it was taken.
G — Suggested build order

Where to start on Monday.

  • 1 · Foundations — Load the twelve site colours and upload Fraunces and Inter. Set the ten text theme styles. Do this before placing a single element; retrofitting type styles across nine sections is a day of work.
  • 2 · Hero — It sets the standard for the rest. If the hero does not feel right at full width, nothing below it will.
  • 3 · Why Arizona and the stats row — The two sections that carry the credibility of the whole page.
  • 4 · The three CMS sections — Industries, Expansion Stories, Arizona City Guide. Data is already there; this is repeater work.
  • 5 · Journey and Partners — Pure layout and hairlines, no images. Fast.
  • 6 · Insights — Connect to the blog once the four drafts are reviewed.
  • 7 · Closing CTA and footer — Then set every reveal animation last, in one pass, so the timing is consistent.
  • 8 · Mobile — Check the hero eyebrow wrapping, the city strip's horizontal scroll, and the stats going to two columns. Those are the three that break first.
One discipline above all. When something feels unfinished, the instinct is to add — a divider, an icon, a background shape, another line of copy. On this design the fix is almost always more space. Increase the section padding before you add anything.
